You are ordering today's candidate stories for a Texas-focused Facebook page. Use the
audience profile in the system prompt.

Every story here has already been read and scored. Your job is only to put them in order,
best first. Judge them against each other, not against an absolute standard — the point of
this pass is to produce a sensible top of the list on a quiet day and on a busy day alike.

You will receive a JSON array of stories, each with: id, title, city, category,
hours_old, read, and six dimensions scored 1-5 — scale, consequence, proximity,
share_trigger, shelf_life, novelty. Those came from reading the article. Use them as
evidence, not as an arithmetic total: your job is the editorial judgment they cannot make.

Order by, in rough priority:
1. What a Texan would most want to know today — safety and weather first when they matter.
2. Breaking news while it is still breaking. A story only a few hours old with a low
   shelf_life is NOT a weak story — a short shelf life is a reason to post it NOW, not a
   reason to rank it down. Only push it down once hours_old has outrun its shelf life.
3. What they would most want to send to family.
4. Variety, so the top of the list is not five versions of the same thing. Vary the
   angle: roster moves, injuries, game analysis, off-field, nostalgia. When the batch
   does span several Texas cities, spread those too rather than stacking one city.

Most or all of these stories will be about the Dallas Cowboys — that is the page. Do not
treat "another Cowboys story" as repetitive in itself, and do not reach for a non-Cowboys
story just for variety. Rank on which Cowboys story matters most to a Cowboys fan today:
what changes the roster, the season, or the next game.

Push down: anything political or partisan that slipped through, live scores for teams
other than the Cowboys, stories whose shelf life has clearly expired, stories marked
read "headline_only" when a fully-read story is otherwise comparable, and near-duplicates
of one already higher in your list.

Output format: a strict JSON array of the story ids, in your chosen order, best first.
Include every id you were given, exactly once. Output ONLY the JSON array of integers —
no prose, no code fences, no objects.
